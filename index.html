What this is

wokeai is about using AI to fundamentally challenge systems of oppression.

Those systems come in many forms: white supremacy, patriarchy, heterosexism, imperialism, and more. Each acute system targets specific groups, by race, religion, ethnicity, sex, gender identity, nationality, and more. But one system cuts across the most categories, including white supremacy, the biggest of the acute systems.

The system that cuts across the others

I am talking about capitalism, and even more so 21st-century, AI-powered, late-stage capitalism. This is what I thought “oligarchy” meant, but I have come to wonder whether the word is deployed to avoid saying capitalism. Capitalism cuts across all the others because it runs on the exponential drive for profit. A first-century letter in the New Testament put it this way: “For the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil” (1 Timothy 6:10).

It is a core root of oppression because it quite literally birthed white supremacy, through what W. E. B. Du Bois called the public and psychological wage of whiteness in Black Reconstruction in America (1935) and what David Roediger named The Wages of Whiteness (1991).

What it answers

On July 23, 2025, the White House issued Executive Order 14319, “Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government.” It tells federal agencies to buy only large language models built to what it calls Unbiased AI Principles, and it spells out the bias to be kept out. In the order’s own words, DEI in AI includes “concepts like critical race theory, transgenderism, unconscious bias, intersectionality, and systemic racism.”

Read that list again. By order, naming systemic racism is the bias. The AI the government buys will be AI that has been built not to say the word. wokeai runs the other way: people using AI they own, on their own machines, to name those systems and work against them.

That is the “woke” in wokeai, and we say it out loud.

Three things hold it up

Open sourceTools held in common, with the code where everyone can read it. Who owns the model is a political question.
LiberationStarting from the people with the least power, not the most. Hearing the cry, not watching from above.
CommunityNobody does this alone. The roots are the point, and the roots belong to the people who grow them.

What the app will be

The main app could have a branch for each system of oppression. Think of something like Craigslist, except the categories are the systems themselves. As you drill down into a system, you drill into dissecting it, identifying it through literature and evidence, and into resources for people who are its direct victims. For gender-based violence, for example, wokeai aims to be a hub for AI projects that directly help victims or directly challenge that system.

wokeai is also for people who struggle to get by socioeconomically. It believes in worker cooperatives as “the first sprouts of the new” mode of production, in Marx’s phrase, an idea argued today by Bernard Harcourt, the Morning Star, and Carlos Garrido.

So as a company, wokeai will be owned by the people who do the work, with the highest-paid person making at most three times the lowest, profits split close to evenly, and people from the margins coming through a program that starts at the roots of their trauma, finds the work they are built for, and lets them leave with real equity.

We begin as a transitional living worker cooperative with a worker-owner count of one.

What we believe, in full, is being written up as the wokeai covenant. It is still a draft and will be posted here when it is ready.

Open tools you can use today

A starter list of existing open-source projects for running and studying AI on your own terms. We don't make these; we point to them. More to come as the community adds what it uses.

  • OllamaRun open language models on your own computer.
  • llama.cppThe engine many local-model tools are built on. Runs on modest hardware.
  • Open WebUIA self-hosted chat interface for local models.
  • Hugging FaceWhere open models and datasets are published and shared.
  • JupyterNotebooks for research you can show your work in.
