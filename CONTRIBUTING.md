# Contributing to wokeai

wokeai is in its first days. Right now the project is a one-page site, two marks, and a short list of open tools. Contributions that help most are new tools for the list, corrections, and bug reports on the site.

By taking part you agree to the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Propose a tool for the list

The list on the site points to existing open-source projects for running and studying AI on your own terms. We don't make these; we point to them.

Open an issue titled `Tool: <name>` and include:

- **Name and link.** The project's own site or repository.
- **One line on what it does**, written for someone who has never heard of it.
- **License.** The tool must be open source (an OSI-approved license, or source available under terms that let anyone run, study, change, and share it). Say which.
- **Why it belongs here.** How does it help a person use their own AI, on their own hardware or under their own control?
- **Your relationship to it**, if any. Maintainers and contributors are welcome to propose their own work; just say so.

A tool goes on the list once a maintainer confirms the license and the link. If a tool later closes its source or shuts down, open an issue so it can be removed.

## Report a problem

Open an issue. For the site, say what you expected, what happened, and what browser or device you were using. For a broken or wrong link, name the tool and paste the link. For anything about the name, the marks, or the words on the page, say which file and quote the line.

## Changes to the site or the repo

Small fixes (typos, broken links, accessibility) can come straight in as a pull request. For anything larger, open an issue first so the change can be talked through before the work is done.

A few things are decided and not open for pull requests: the name **wokeai**, its spoken form, the marks in `marks/`, and the site copy. The README's "Decisions, dated" section records what was decided and when. If you think a decision is wrong, open an issue and make the case; decisions get revisited, they just don't get silently overwritten.

## Commit messages

Commit messages here are written as memory for whoever reads the history later: what changed, why, and what was decided. Say that in the body, not just which files moved.
